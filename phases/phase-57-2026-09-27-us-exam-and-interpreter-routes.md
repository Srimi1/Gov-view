## Language assessment and appointment kind

The court system's [exam process guidance](https://www.nycourts.gov/careers/exams/exams-process-and-content) distinguishes Spanish written testing from other-language English/legal-terminology testing. Oral interpreting assesses sight, consecutive and simultaneous modes, with language-specific availability, abbreviated routes and reference alternatives. Hindi, Japanese, Punjabi and Urdu do not automatically share an identical full assessment. No CEFR/JLPT equivalence is invented.

Current interpreter information requires high-school diploma/equivalent, legal US work eligibility and criminal-history screening before assignments, with additional applicable training. Recognized RID credentials apply to ASL; the court system says it does not administer ASL proficiency testing. This private credential is relevant because the official pathway recognizes it, not because every private language exam belongs in scope.

The [linked Candidate Guide](https://www.nycourts.gov/legacyPDFs/COURTINTERPRETER/Candidate_Guide.pdf) describes independent contractors and explicitly distinguishes screening/registry inclusion from professional certification. It lists **$385 full-day/$220 half-day**, while the current webpage lists **$500/$300**. Rates require authority reconciliation; none is selected for publication. Registry inclusion is not guaranteed work, a permanent staff appointment or a professional licence. Older staff eligible-list references are not new openings.
